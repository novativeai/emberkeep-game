## 1. What actually happened

Sixty years ago, **Silas** kept the library at the Moonhold — the house of the
Daughters of the Moon. He had no magic. Not a spark, not once. He married into
the order, his wife died young, and he spent the rest of his life reading every
account of loss the Hold owned, looking for a single case of something taken
that was ever given back. He never found one.

His elder daughter **Eleanor** had the exact craft that question needed. The
Daughters' magic does not burn — *it gives back light it was given.* It can
**catch** a thing, **hold** it, and **return** it. Nothing else.

At nineteen, at the end of her formation, Eleanor performed her final rite on
the altar at Emberkeep. It was called **the Keeping**. She caught the Great
Flame and sealed it in a vessel — **the Lantern** — and she believed, entirely,
that they would give it back at dawn. She had never been so good at anything.
Her father watched, and for the first time in her life he looked at her.

He did not give it back. He left with the Lantern.

**The dragons did not die.** Without the Flame they went cold and went to sleep,
and the isle went to ash.

And nobody could wake them. Not Eleanor, not the order, not anyone — because
**the Flame answers a Keeper's hands.** There was a line of people who tended the
sanctuary, and it never stopped knowing them. Catching fire is craft, and Eleanor
is the best there has ever been at it. **Giving it back is not craft at all**, and
no amount of skill substitutes.

**The player is the last of that line, and does not know it.** That is the hidden
power the whole campaign turns on: they can wake what is sleeping, and nobody
else alive can. Eleanor spent sixty years looking for them — not for a helper,
but for the only thing in the world that could undo what she did.

That is why she needs the Keeper, and why she found them before she ever
introduced herself.

## 2. Where it all is now

- **Silas** — gone. Eleanor stopped looking a long time ago; that is the *"So
  did I, once"* in the opening.
- **The Lantern** — in the north. **Selyna** has it. She recovered it, and she
  has spent decades keeping the Flame alive in the Borealis cold, because that
  is her craft: Eleanor *returns*, Selyna **preserves**.
- **The Borealis dragons** are alive and awake because they followed the Flame
  north and Selyna has kept it burning. That is the in-fiction reason world 2
  has a living dragon population and Emberkeep has none.
- **Returning it requires a Keeper's hands.** Selyna can keep the Flame burning
  forever and still not give it back; that is the difference between preserving a
  thing and healing with it. Nobody had a Keeper. That is the whole reason a
  stranger's arrival changes everything, and it is why *both* sisters need the
  player.

## 3. The three lies Eleanor tells, and why they are not lies

She never states a falsehood. She lets true sentences be misheard. This is the
authoring rule — **unreliable about herself, never about the rules** — and it
means every reveal recontextualises rather than retracts.

| What she says | What the player hears | What is true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *"the ash under your hands went warm"* | A curiosity | She had been hunting that exact sign for sixty years |
| *"So did I, once."* (want to know what happened) | She researched it and gave up | She already knows. She was holding the vessel. |
| *"I can't wake any of it."* | A mage's limitation | The precise shape of her punishment: she can take, and cannot give back. |

**The cruellest one is free.** She christens the player **Keeper** in the first
minute, and her rite was called **the Keeping**. She gives them the name of her
crime as a title, on purpose, and cannot explain why for weeks. Chapter 7 is
where she finally says so, and it re-reads the opening for anyone paying
attention.

## 4. Eleanor's arc — the pattern she is repeating

This is the spine, and it is what keeps her from being a woman with a sad
backstory rather than a character.

> Silas found someone with a power he did not have, and used it without telling
> her all of it. Eleanor found someone with a power **they did not know they
> had**, and is using it without telling them all of it.

**She knows.** It is the thing she is most afraid of, and it is why she is warm
but keeps her distance, why she teaches so plainly, why she flinches at the word
Keeper. Her turn is not a confession under pressure — it is her **breaking the
pattern on purpose**: she tells the Keeper the entire truth *before* asking for
the last thing, which is the one courtesy her father never paid her.

Everything she is granted afterwards, she is granted by a player who could have
walked.

## 5. Cast

**Eleanor** — elder sister, ~19 at the rite, so early eighties and Moon-slowed
now; reads as the player's age. Composed, precise, modern speech, no archaisms.
Oblique about herself, flat and clear about rules. Signature: she begins
something true, hears it, and substitutes something safer — an em-dash and a
subject change. Warm, and keeps her hands to herself.

**Selyna** — younger sister, was too young to be at the rite. **She is never in
Emberkeep.** She is in the north with her own sanctuary and her own dragons, and
the player only ever meets her there. In world 1 she exists entirely as
handwriting. Clipped, dry,
unsentimental, and wounded in a way she refuses to perform. She is **not the
villain and must never be written as one**: she is the one who carried the thing
for sixty years while her sister read about it. Her refusal has a condition, not
a price — she will return the Flame when Eleanor says aloud, in front of the
dragons and the Keeper, what she did. Not as punishment. Because a thing given
back by someone who won't name the taking is not given back.

**The Golden Elder** — the sole living witness; she was awake on the altar and
saw both of them. Silent for all of world 1 until she wakes. Grand, slow,
capital-letter register — she is the only character permitted it. She does not
accuse; she **testifies**, and then hands the verdict to the player.

**The Keeper (player)** — silent, always. Eleanor and Selyna must voice the
player's questions and be seen doing it: *"You'll want to know…"*, *"Don't ask
me that yet."* The Keeper's silence is an asset — it makes them the only person
in the story with nothing to defend.

## 6. The reveal ladder

Each rung is gated on a system milestone, so story is **paid out by play**. No
rung may be reached early; every rung recontextualises the ones below it.

| Ch | Gate | The rung |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tutorial | Something overruled her spell. *"I was—"* |
| 2 | First Ledger order | Her craft: catch, hold, return. (The murder weapon, presented as modesty.) |
| 3 | First dragon named | They didn't die — they *slept*. Names were never taken. |
| 4 | 2nd region restored | She cannot wake anything. The player can — and does, on screen. |
| 5 | Trust 2 | There is a sister, and she is hostile. |
| 6 | Dragon Book ×5 | Her father. A librarian with no magic, looking for one thing given back. |
| 7 | 2nd dragon named | There was a rite. She performed it. It was called **the Keeping**. |
| 8 | 3rd region / Trust 4 | It was never luck. She knew what the player was, went looking, and used them. |
| 9 | **Golden Elder wakes** | The witness testifies. She was nineteen, she was crying, she did it anyway. |
| 10 | Hub opens (south) | She breaks the pattern: the whole truth, unasked, before the last request. |
| 11 | Borealis opens (north) | Selyna has the Lantern, and has had it for decades. |
| 12 | Selyna's terms | Return is possible, and it costs Eleanor the only thing she has left. |

**The withheld thing must always be the next rung, never a random secret.** A
mystery that pays out on a schedule the player can feel is a retention spine; one
that pays out arbitrarily is just an author being coy.

## 7. Where the words actually live

Chapter beats alone will not fill weeks. The volume comes from systems that
**already speak**, re-voiced per story stage — the same Ledger says new things in
chapter 6 than it did in chapter 2, at no design cost.

| Channel | Volume | Why it carries story cheaply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chapter beats** | 12 × 5–8 bubbles | The ladder itself |
| **Ledger banter** | 5 lines × 9 stages | Already fires on `order:completed`; swap the bank by chapter |
| **Day-phase lines** | 4 phases × 3 stages | The four-phase clock (merge-chains §3) gives presence for almost nothing |
| **Naming ceremony** | ~6 per dragon | The single most emotional moment the game has |
| **Trust milestones** | 5 per dragon + Eleanor's commentary | Conduct, not a bar — she narrates what you'd otherwise miss |
| **Dragon Book marginalia** | 1 per entry | **Eleanor annotates the book.** The best hiding place in the game: guilt in one-line doses, deniable, re-readable |
| **Selyna's letters** | 5, world 1 | Establishes the antagonist-who-isn't before she appears |
| **The Elder** | ~8, post-wake | Rare and heavy. Her scarcity is the point |

**The Dragon Book marginalia is the highest-value channel and the least
obvious.** A pokédex the player opens voluntarily, hundreds of times, is the
only place you can put a sentence that means nothing on first read and everything
on the fourth. Put the guilt there in fragments and let the player assemble it
before any chapter beat confirms it.

## 8. Rules for writing more of this

1. **Proper-noun budget stays tight.** Canon nouns: *Emberkeep · Borealis · the
   Moonhold ("the Hold") · Daughters of the Moon · the Great Flame · the
   Keeping · the Lantern · Silas.* Eight, across a campaign. Do not add a ninth
   without retiring one.
2. **Never explain a system and a feeling in the same breath.** Teaching lines
   are flat; story lines are sideways. The player learns the difference by ear.
3. **Reveals land on the player's action, not on a timer.** She reacts to what
   they did; she does not announce chapters.
4. **Selyna never gloats and Eleanor never grovels.** The moment either does,
   the story becomes a melodrama and the sisters become a plot device.
5. **The dragons are not symbols.** They are animals with preferences. They must
   never be seen to understand the plot — except the Elder, who is a person.
6. **Nothing load-bearing is text-only.** Assume the player skipped it. Every
   reveal must also be legible from the board, the art, or a system's behaviour.

## 9. Decisions taken here that are open to veto

These were invented to make the brief coherent; the brief itself (father, her
magic, her ignorance, Selyna's refusal) is untouched.

- **Silas has no magic**, which is *why* he needed his daughter — and is what
  makes Eleanor's use of the Keeper a repetition rather than a coincidence.
- **His motive is grief, not power.** His wife died; he wanted proof that what is
  taken can be returned. It makes the theft a rehearsal and keeps him off the
  villain shelf.
- **The player's hidden power is inherited, not chosen** — they are the last of
  the line that tended the Flame, and they do not know it. Inheritance keeps the
  chosen-one flattery out: nobody picked them, and the power is a fact about
  their hands rather than a destiny about their soul.
- **Eleanor found them deliberately.** Not luck, not coincidence — which is what
  makes her a mirror of her father rather than merely a woman with a sad past.
- **Selyna holds the Lantern** and has kept the Flame alive in the cold. This
  gives world 2 an engine, explains why Borealis has living dragons, and makes
  the sisters' reconciliation the gate on the ending rather than a cutscene.
- **The rite was called the Keeping**, so the player's title is the name of the
  crime. Free, and it re-reads the opening.
