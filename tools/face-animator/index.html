Step 1 — Load Your Sprite
Upload the character's portrait. It should show the neutral resting face: mouth closed, eyes open, relaxed expression.
How this tool works: This uses replacement animation — the industry-standard technique in Disney/Warner 2D animation, visual novels, and game cut-scenes.
Instead of warping pixels, you supply hand-drawn replacement images for each expression state (closed eyes, open mouth shapes). The engine composites them over the base sprite at the position you mark. This handles any perspective correctly — because you drew the shapes at the right angle.

Step 2 — Define the Perspective
This affects the instructions you receive in the next step for marking regions, and how overlays should be drawn.
Why this matters: Most character sprites are not straight-on. Visual novel portraits, game dialogue busts, and cut-scene characters are typically in 3/4 view — the face turned 30–45° to one side. In 3/4 view the far eye is smaller (foreshortening), the mouth axis is tilted, and the near cheek is wider. All mouth-shape overlays should be drawn to match this angle — which you already know because you drew the sprite.

Step 4 — Eye Blink Setup
Choose how closed eyes are rendered during the blink animation.
Industry standard: In hand-drawn 2D animation, the "blink frame" is simply a separate drawing of closed eyes. The engine cross-fades from open (base sprite) → closed (overlay) → open. This works perfectly regardless of perspective — you draw the eyelids at the correct angle for your character.

Step 5 — Mouth Shapes (Visemes)
Upload a transparent overlay PNG for each mouth position. Minimum: REST, A, O for basic lip sync. Upload all 8 for professional quality.
Preston Blair phoneme set — the industry standard used in Disney, Hanna-Barbera, anime, and most video games.
Each overlay is the same size as your sprite, with only the mouth pixels on a transparent background. Draw each mouth shape at the correct perspective angle and position for your character's face — you know the angle because you drew the sprite.

Smoothness comes from cross-fading, not from more shapes. The preview engine cross-fades between consecutive phoneme frames over ~55ms. This turns 8 hard poses into fluid, natural-looking speech — the same technique used in Cuphead, Hades, and most professional 2D games. Drag the Phoneme crossfade slider in Step 6 to feel the difference.
How to draw mouth overlays:
1. Open your sprite in your drawing app
2. Create a new layer on top
3. Draw the mouth shape in the exact position it sits on the face
4. For 3/4 perspective: the mouth corners follow the face's foreshortening — the far corner is slightly higher/smaller
5. Export only the mouth layer as transparent PNG (same dimensions as sprite)
6. Repeat for each shape you want to support
